Free QR & Barcode Maker for Authors

Three small makers for the things that go on a book: a QR code, an ISBN barcode, and an optional price strip. Everything happens in your browser. Nothing you type or upload is sent to us or stored. These tools turn information you already have into a picture. They don’t write anything for your book.

  • QR code. A web address drawn as a picture a phone can read.
  • ISBN barcode. Your book’s ISBN drawn as bars so retailers’ scanners can read it. The ISBN is the number; the barcode is just that number drawn as bars. This page draws an ISBN you already own. It never issues or looks up ISBNs.
  • Price add-on. An optional five-digit strip printed beside the ISBN barcode.

QR code maker

Type the web address you want the code to open. This makes a static code: the address is baked into the picture.

Before you print one: a printed QR code points at an address, not at a page. The page can change, but if the address ever dies (a domain lapses, a page moves, a site is rebuilt), every printed copy points at nothing, and it can’t be fixed. Point the code at a page on a domain you control for the long term, such as a /book page on your own site, and repoint that page whenever the destination changes.

Black on white, with the blank border (the quiet zone) included. Low contrast is the commonest reason scans fail.

Error correction: M

Optional: add your own logo to the center

The image stays on your device. Adding a logo switches the code to its strongest error correction and keeps the logo to about a fifth of the code’s width, on a white backing.

A logo still reduces scan margin. Test at the smallest size you’ll print, in poor light, on more than one phone.

ISBN barcode maker

Enter an ISBN-13 (starting 978 or 979). Hyphens and spaces are fine. If you enter a valid ISBN-10, we’ll convert it to ISBN-13 and tell you.

The barcode is only as correct as the number you typed. A wrong barcode on a printed run is expensive. Confirm the number against your ISBN agency record, and ask your printer for their final specs before you print.

The number is printed beneath the bars. Books often also print the hyphenated ISBN above the barcode. Your ISBN agency record or your printer can give you the right hyphenation. Plain digits under the bars are fine.

Price add-on (optional)

Most authors should skip this. The add-on is a five-digit strip beside the ISBN barcode. The first digit is the currency and the other four are the price in cents, so a US$12.99 book is 51299. The “no price” code, 90000, is the safe default.
Caution: the add-on bakes the price into every printed copy. If you change the price later, the printed books are wrong. Retailers often apply their own pricing anyway, so this is mostly useful for direct sales (your own site, events).
