Connecting your web address at VentraIP

Your web address lives at VentraIP, and your website lives at GitHub. This short guide shows you how to point one at the other, so that typing your address opens your site.

Step 01

What you're doing, and what to expect

A web address is like a street name, and a DNS record is a signpost that says which building it leads to. You'll add a handful of signposts at VentraIP that point to your site's home at GitHub. That's all this is.

  • VentraIP doesn't connect it for you. Registering the address doesn't point it anywhere useful. You add the records yourself, once, and they stay put.
  • It takes about 15 minutes to enter the records, then a wait. VentraIP says changes usually show up within a few hours, and GitHub occasionally takes up to a day.
  • Nothing here can break your site. It isn't switched on yet, or, if it is, a wrong record just means the address doesn't open it until we fix it.

You don't have to do this alone. Call or text me, or share your screen on a video call, and we'll do it together. This guide is here so you can see what's coming and follow along.

Never send me your VentraIP password. I never need it. If a step needs something only you can do, I'll talk you through it.

Step 02

Before you start

Have these ready

  • Your VentraIP login (the one you use for VIPcontrol, VentraIP's account area).
  • Your web address, spelled exactly as it appears in your VentraIP domain list. Check the ending too (.com, .com.au, .au and so on), because it's easy to remember a shorter version than the one you actually own.
  • Your GitHub username.
  • GitHub's verification record. You get it in your GitHub guide, Step 05, part A, when you add your web address on your account's Pages screen. Leave that GitHub page open if you can.
  • Your web address already saved on your repository's Pages screen (Step 05, part B of the GitHub guide). GitHub asks for this before the records go in, so no one else can claim your address.

Check this before Step 03: do you use email at this web address, such as you@yourdomain.com, or does anything else already use it? Tell me before you open any settings at VentraIP, and wait for my go-ahead.

Email depends on records of its own. We'll leave those exactly as they are, but a wrong click at VentraIP could switch them off, so it's worth a quick check first.

Step 03

Open your DNS settings

  1. Log in to VIPcontrol at VentraIP.
  2. Choose My Services, then the Domains tab.
  3. Find your web address in the list and choose it to open the page for that domain.
  4. In the menu down the left-hand side, choose Manage DNS.
  5. Across the top of the page you'll see a row of tabs: VentraIP Hosting, Custom Nameservers, DNS Hosting, Forwarding and Park Domain. Choose the DNS Hosting tab. A new domain often shows Custom Nameservers as the current setting. That's normal, so don't worry about it.
  6. The DNS Hosting box at the top of the page has an Enable button. Choose it.
  7. A screen headed Current DNS settings will be overridden appears, listing your old nameservers and the new ones. This is expected. If the two lists are the same (a new domain is usually already on VentraIP's own, ns1, ns2 and ns3.nameserver.net.au), nothing is lost. Tick the box saying you understand DNS propagation may take up to an hour, then choose Set DNS.
  8. You should now see a table of records, with a row along the top for adding new ones. That's where everything below happens.

Ignore the Create New Records button lower down the page. It belongs to Child Nameservers, which you don't need, and it isn't where DNS records go. Your records table is in the DNS Hosting box at the top.

Step 04

Clear out old records

Your address may already have records pointing somewhere else, such as a placeholder page. They'd compete with the new ones, so they go first.

  1. Look down the table for any A or AAAA record with a blank hostname, and any A or CNAME record whose hostname is www.
  2. For each one, choose the red x beside it to remove it.
  3. Leave everything else alone, especially any MX records (these run email) and anything you don't recognise.

On a new domain the table usually holds just five rows: two A records pointing at 103.42.108.46 (one for the bare address, one for www) and three NS records. Remove the two A records and leave the three NS records. A new domain has no MX record.

Deleting isn't instant, and VentraIP doesn't tell you it has worked. The red x has no Save button and no confirmation. The row only disappears after you've refreshed the page, or left it and reopened it, sometimes a few times. Refresh before you decide it hasn't worked.

This matters later: the www record in Step 05 can't be added until the old www A record has really gone.

Not sure whether a record is safe to remove? Leave it, and send me a screenshot of the table. A screenshot of DNS records contains no passwords, so it's safe to share.

Step 05

Add the records that point to GitHub

You'll add five records in all. Each is added from the row along the top of the table: set the Type, fill in the Hostname and Value, then choose the plus icon at the end of the row.

The five records to add
TypeHostnameValue
ALeave blank185.199.108.153
ALeave blank185.199.109.153
ALeave blank185.199.110.153
ALeave blank185.199.111.153
CNAMEwwwyour-username.github.io (your GitHub username, then .github.io)
  • Choose the Type first. The Type box starts on A. If you leave it there and type something like your-username.github.io, VentraIP says Must be a valid IPv4 address. For the CNAME, change the Type to CNAME before you fill in anything else.
  • Hostname: leave it blank, don't type @. Other registrars use an @ sign for "the main address". VentraIP uses an empty box. Your web address sits greyed out at the end of the Hostname box, so blank means the bare address, and for www you type only www.
  • Leave the TTL as it is. The default is right.
  • Leave Priority empty. It's only for email records.
  • Copy and paste the values rather than typing them. One wrong digit is easy to make and hard to spot.
  • For the CNAME value, use your username only, ending in .github.io. Don't add your repository's name.

There are four A records because GitHub has four servers, and it uses whichever is free. All four should be there.

If VentraIP says a record already exists, it may simply mean you've already added it. Refresh and look in the table before you try again. If it's the www record that's refused, the old www A record from Step 04 probably hasn't gone yet. Refresh until it has.

Step 06

Add the verification record

One more record proves to GitHub that the address is really yours.

  1. Back on your GitHub page (Step 05, part A of the GitHub guide), find the TXT record GitHub shows you.
  2. At VentraIP, set the Type to TXT.
  3. For Hostname, enter _github-pages-challenge-your-username, using your GitHub username. Type only this much, without your web address on the end (the Hostname box adds it for you). Capital letters don't matter.
  4. For Value, paste the code GitHub gave you, then choose the plus icon.

Here is what the finished row looks like, using the made-up username janesmith. Yours will have your own username and your own code.

A finished TXT row (example)
TypeHostnameValue
TXT_github-pages-challenge-janesmiththe long code from GitHub, pasted with GitHub's copy button

The name goes in Hostname. The code goes in Value. They are easy to swap. If the name ends up in the Value box and Hostname is left blank, the record sits at your main address and does nothing, and GitHub will say it can't find your record. If that happens, don't worry and don't try to fix the old row. Add a new TXT record with the right details in the right boxes, and send me a screenshot of the table.

Keep this record for good. GitHub needs it to go on recognising your address as yours. Please don't delete it later, even though it looks like leftover clutter.

Step 07

Wait, check, and what to send me

What your finished table should look like

Before you wait, check your table against this list. It should hold exactly:

  • Four A records with a blank hostname, pointing at the four 185.199.… addresses
  • One CNAME record for www, pointing at your-username.github.io
  • One TXT record whose Hostname starts with an underscore: _github-pages-challenge- followed by your username.
  • No TXT row with a blank Hostname. If a TXT row has nothing in Hostname and the text _github-pages-challenge-… in Value, the name and the code have been swapped. See the warning in Step 06.
  • Three NS records, left as they were
  • No rows pointing at 103.42.108.46

Wait a little

VentraIP says new records usually take a few hours to spread across the internet. Occasionally, GitHub takes up to a day. Go and make a cup of tea. Nothing needs doing in the meantime.

Check it worked

  1. Open your repository's Pages screen (your repository, then Settings, then Pages). Once things are connected, GitHub shows DNS check successful.
  2. Open your web address in your browser. Your site should appear.
  3. On your account's Pages screen (your profile menu, then Settings, then Pages), choose Verify beside your address if you haven't yet.
  4. Last, back on your repository's Pages screen, tick Enforce HTTPS to get the padlock. It can take up to a day to become available, and I'll remind you.

Checklist

  • A note that all six records are added (four A, one CNAME, one TXT)
  • A screenshot of the DNS table, if you'd like me to double-check it. It contains no passwords.
  • Anything on screen that didn't match this guide, even if you worked around it

Looking after your address

  • Keep your web address renewed at VentraIP. If it lapses, your site goes offline until it's renewed. This is the one recurring cost you'll have, and it's yours to manage. I never handle it.
  • Keep your email address current in VIPcontrol, because that's where VentraIP sends renewal reminders.
  • Check with me before changing DNS records. The six you added are all that your site needs.

If anything on screen doesn't match this guide, stop and ask. VentraIP moves its menus around from time to time, and a quick question beats a wrong turn.
